## Method

For each real tariff change below, the agent was given only Federal Register documents published **before** the date the change was signed (or, for agency notices, took effect). An event is a **hit** when, on the day before the change, a pending notice on the same measure matched at least one of the affected sample products. Lead time runs from the first such pending notice to the change date. "Likelihood at first warning" is the reader's 90-day estimate on that first notice; "likelihood on the eve" is the aged estimate the day before the change (see `tariff_agent/scoring.py`).

## How to read these results

- **Lead time is long but early likelihoods are low.** Investigations are public for months, so the agent flags products early; but a freshly opened investigation rarely ends within 90 days, so the first-warning likelihood is deliberately small. The useful signal is the flag plus a likelihood that rises as statutory deadlines approach.
- **The hazard table is in-sample.** The ageing rule in `scoring.py` was set from the same 2025-2026 Section 232 investigations that appear in this backtest, so eve-of-change likelihoods and the false-alarm count are optimistic. A fair test needs future events.
- **Small sample.** Eight events and one sample importer. Treat the hit rate as an illustration, not a statistic.
- **LLM hindsight.** When the LLM reader is used, the model may already know how these events ended. The prompt restricts it to the notice text, but that cannot be verified from outside.
- **Publication lag.** Proclamations are usually posted by the White House a few days before they appear in the Federal Register; dates here use the signing date for the change and Federal Register publication dates for what the agent could see.
